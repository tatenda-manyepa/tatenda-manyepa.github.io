Operational Controls

Planned evidence showing how monitoring, security controls, auditability, cost ownership and responsible AI governance make the target solution diagnosable, accountable and safe to operate.

Why This Phase Exists

The current-state assessment identified reactive troubleshooting, limited operational visibility and dependence on manual intervention. The target solution therefore needs more than functional components: teams must be able to see what is happening, understand failures, control access and demonstrate who or what performed important actions.

Business Problem

Operational failures can take too long to diagnose when evidence is fragmented and support teams depend on manual investigation.

Target Outcome

Build operational visibility and governance into the platform so incidents, security events, AI behaviour and cost can be monitored and reviewed.

Control Principle

Controls are designed with the solution rather than added after deployment, reducing operational and governance gaps.

Human Accountability

Automation and AI assist people rather than silently becoming operational authorities; important actions remain traceable and reviewable.

Proposed Operational Control Loop

The planned evidence follows a simple control loop from system activity through telemetry and detection to human-reviewed response and retained evidence.

01

System Activity

Applications, integrations and AI-assisted workflows perform controlled actions.

02

Capture Evidence

Logs, metrics and relevant audit events are collected centrally.

03

Detect

CloudWatch alarms and defined thresholds identify abnormal conditions.

04

Notify & Review

Relevant teams receive alerts and review the supporting evidence.

05

Respond & Improve

Actions are taken, recorded and used to improve controls and operating knowledge.

Planned Control Evidence

The prototype will focus on controls that directly address the operational risks identified in the current state.

Monitoring & Logging

Evidence: Amazon CloudWatch logs, metrics and alarms showing application health, integration failures and operational events.

Alerting

Evidence: Amazon SNS notifications triggered by meaningful operational thresholds rather than relying solely on manual observation.

IAM Boundaries

Evidence: Least-privilege roles and permissions demonstrating controlled access between people, services and data.

Secrets Handling

Evidence: Sensitive configuration and credentials are separated from application code and accessed through controlled mechanisms.

Audit Evidence

Evidence: Traceable records for important administrative, service and AI-assisted actions so decisions can be reviewed.

Cost Ownership

Evidence: Basic tagging, service ownership and cost visibility so the architecture can be assessed against operational value as well as technical capability.

AI Quality Checks

Evidence: Source grounding, citations, feedback and human validation for the AI Knowledge Assistant rather than unrestricted automated action.

Recovery Evidence

Evidence: Defined failure and recovery scenarios demonstrating that resilience controls can be tested rather than assumed.

Architecture Decision

Observability and governance are part of the architecture

Monitoring, access control, auditability and governance are introduced alongside the platform rather than after deployment. Operational trust is treated as a design requirement because a technically functional solution is not sufficient if teams cannot diagnose failures, explain actions, manage access or safely govern AI-assisted workflows.

Responsible AI Controls

The AI Knowledge Assistant is designed as decision support, so the controls prioritise grounding, transparency and human accountability over autonomous operational action.

Approved Knowledge

Responses are grounded in governed enterprise sources rather than unrestricted operational information.

Source References

Where appropriate, responses expose supporting sources so users can verify the information used.

Human Review

Recommendations support investigation and decision-making; people remain responsible for consequential operational actions.

Feedback & Quality

User feedback and representative test questions provide evidence for improving accuracy and identifying unsafe or weak responses.

Success Criteria

Diagnosable

Failures generate sufficient telemetry for support teams to understand where and why a workflow failed.

Auditable

Important actions and changes leave evidence that can be reviewed after the event.

Least Privilege

Users and services receive only the access required for their responsibilities.

Actionable Alerting

Alerts identify meaningful conditions and provide enough context to begin investigation.

Responsible AI

AI outputs are grounded, reviewable and used as decision support rather than uncontrolled operational authority.

Cost Awareness

Platform components have identifiable ownership and sufficient cost visibility to support architectural trade-offs.

What This Demonstrates

Security by Design

Security boundaries and access decisions are considered as architectural requirements, not implementation afterthoughts.

Operational Visibility

Designing systems so teams can understand service health, failures and dependencies through usable evidence.

Governance

Connecting technology choices to accountability, auditability, operational ownership and controlled change.

Responsible AI Adoption

Applying human oversight, grounding and quality controls to AI capabilities before expanding automation.
