My Enterprise AI Delivery Framework

A practical consulting framework that starts with the business problem, translates needs into an appropriate solution, validates the design before full implementation and measures outcomes after delivery. The approach starts simple, adds complexity only where justified, and uses documentation and stakeholder feedback throughout the lifecycle. Click each card to explore the method.

01

Business Discovery & Requirements

“Start with the workflow, pain point and measurable business outcome.”

Click to learn more →

Business Discovery & Requirements

  • Understand the business problem, current workflow and desired outcomes
  • Identify stakeholders, users and operational pain points
  • Gather and validate functional and non-functional requirements
  • Capture risks, constraints, assumptions and priorities
  • Define success criteria and create an agreed documentation baseline

Deliverables: Business Discovery, Current-State Assessment, Requirements Specification and Stakeholder Feedback Log.

02

Solution Architecture

“Design the solution around the business requirements, then choose the technology that best supports them.”

Click to learn more →

Solution Architecture

  • Translate business requirements into solution capabilities and architecture drivers
  • Design the simplest viable target architecture that solves the problem
  • Select the right technologies, platforms and integration patterns for the requirements
  • Design security, data, resilience and governance controls from the start
  • Evaluate alternatives, risks and trade-offs, then document why decisions were made
  • Design for evolution so complexity is added only when new requirements justify it

Deliverables: Target Architecture, Technology Selection, Architecture Decision Records and Updated Solution Documentation.

03

Proof of Concept

“Test the proposed solution and highest-risk assumptions before committing to full implementation.”

Click to learn more →

Proof of Concept

  • Build a focused proof of concept for the highest-priority part of the solution
  • Validate technical feasibility, architecture and integration assumptions
  • Test security, usability, performance and operational fit at an appropriate level
  • Use AI only where it offers clear benefit to the workflow or decision process
  • Gather stakeholder feedback, document findings and refine the design before scaling

Deliverables: Working Proof of Concept, Validation Findings, Feedback Record and Updated Architecture.

04

Delivery, Adoption & Business Outcomes

“Successful architecture is measured by business outcomes, not technology.”

Click to learn more →

Delivery, Adoption & Business Outcomes

  • Define testing, deployment, rollback and release strategy
  • Plan monitoring, support, ownership and operational readiness
  • Support adoption and change management with clear stakeholder communication
  • Measure KPIs, usefulness, business impact and operational performance
  • Maintain documentation and feed customer/user feedback into continuous improvement
  • Capture lessons learned and evolve the roadmap as requirements change

Deliverables: Implementation Plan, Operational Documentation, Business Outcomes Report, Feedback Loop and Future Roadmap.

Lifecycle principles: Keep the first solution as simple as the requirements allow, document decisions as the architecture evolves, and maintain a continuous stakeholder feedback loop from discovery through implementation and ongoing improvement.

1. Business Discovery

Discover → Understand the operational problem, stakeholders, workflow and success criteria.

Apex Operational Systems Ltd operates a legacy fulfilment platform affected by recurring outages, manual investigation, fragmented knowledge and limited resilience. The engagement focuses on reducing operational disruption through a more resilient cloud architecture and a practical AI-assisted support workflow.

Operational Stability Issues

Recurring high-severity outages disrupt robotic movement, stock allocation, and order picking processes across fulfilment operations.

Limited High Availability & DR

Manual standby infrastructure and limited automated failover capability increase recovery times and create single points of failure.

Reactive Support Model

Issues are typically identified after service degradation or failure, with limited proactive alerting and slow root cause analysis.

Siloed Teams & Knowledge Gaps

Development, testing, and support operate independently, creating poor cross-team visibility and inconsistent troubleshooting processes.

On-Premises Infrastructure Constraints

Database timeouts, network instability, maintenance overhead, and limited horizontal scalability affect peak operational periods.

Deployment & Change Risk

Hotfixes and changes require stronger CI/CD controls, validation, rollback planning, and post-release monitoring.

2. Requirements & Opportunity Analysis

Analyse → Convert business pain points into requirements, risks, priorities and architecture drivers.

Three use cases were prioritised because they address clear operational problems, can be introduced with human oversight, and provide measurable value without attempting to automate high-risk decisions.

Priority 1

Knowledge Assistant

Uses trusted runbooks, known issues and architecture documents to answer operational questions and reduce time spent searching for information.

Why first: High value, manageable risk and a clear path to a practical prototype.
Priority 2

Incident Investigation Assistant

Summarises alerts, retrieves similar incidents and suggests investigation steps while keeping engineers responsible for operational decisions.

Why next: Extends the knowledge assistant into a guided operational workflow.
Priority 3

Automated Operational Reporting

Turns incident and service data into weekly summaries, recurring-issue themes and management-ready recommendations.

Why included: Demonstrates measurable time savings and improved management visibility.
Future roadmap: Ticket classification, release-risk support and low-risk workflow automation remain potential later phases. They are not treated as immediate delivery commitments until the first three use cases prove value.

6. Architecture Decisions

A concise view of the key architecture choices behind the solution. Each decision is based on the problem, alternatives, trade-offs and expected business value.

Why ECS Fargate?

Need: Reliable container hosting without cluster administration.

Decision: ECS Fargate.
Trade-off: Less host-level control than Kubernetes, but lower operational complexity.

Why RDS Multi-AZ?

Need: Reduce manual database failover and improve availability.

Decision: Managed relational database with Multi-AZ resilience.
Trade-off: Higher managed-service cost in exchange for lower operational risk.

Why RAG?

Need: Answers grounded in approved operational knowledge.

Decision: Retrieval-augmented generation.
Trade-off: Retrieval quality must be governed and tested, but knowledge can stay current without retraining.

Why Bedrock?

Need: Managed foundation-model access without operating model infrastructure.

Decision: Amazon Bedrock.
Trade-off: Managed convenience over maximum low-level model control.

Why CloudWatch?

Need: Move from reactive support to visible, measurable operations.

Decision: Central metrics, logs and alarms.
Trade-off: Requires alert design and ownership to avoid noise.

Why Terraform?

Need: Repeatable, reviewable infrastructure changes.

Decision: Infrastructure as Code.
Trade-off: Adds code and state management, but improves consistency and traceability.

Why Human Review?

Need: Use AI safely in operational workflows.

Decision: AI supports decisions rather than making high-impact changes autonomously.
Trade-off: Less automation, but stronger safety and accountability.

Why Multi-AZ?

Need: Reduce single points of failure.

Decision: Spread critical services across Availability Zones.
Trade-off: Additional cost for stronger resilience.
