How do you stop a one-person trades business losing jobs while they’re busy doing one?

Delivered for KAI-TEQ, the small technology consultancy where I work part-time as an Solutions Consultant. This page shows how I took it from a customer problem to a product ready for its first customers. Results will be added as they come in.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Every missed call is a job someone else gets

Trades owners can’t answer the phone up a ladder or under a sink. Customers rarely leave voicemails; they ring the next number. Messages pile up, and quotes, invoices and chasing payments all happen in the evening.

  • Lost work Unanswered calls during the working day go to competitors
  • Lost evenings Back-and-forth about times, prices and addresses
  • Root cause The owner is the only person who can reply, and they’re busy working

02 / DISCOVERY

Start with how their day really works

Before designing anything, I spoke to trades business owners about how enquiries arrive and where they get lost. The questions I asked:

  • How do new customers contact you, and what happens when you can’t answer?
  • What details do you need before you can book a job?
  • Which jobs would you happily let something book for you, and which must you see first?
  • What would make you stop trusting an assistant replying in your name?
  • What is one job worth to you, and how many do you think you lose a week?

What I learned: they didn’t want a chatbot. They wanted their phone to keep working while they couldn’t, without losing control of their diary, prices or the areas they cover.

04 / REQUIREMENTS

What it must do first, and what can wait

Must have

  • Reply to WhatsApp and missed calls straight away
  • Collect the job, postcode and preferred time
  • Offer only real slots from the owner’s diary
  • Book only within set hours, areas and prices
  • Owner approval before confirming (until they choose auto-confirm)

Should have

  • Reminder the day before the job
  • Hand over to the owner when a question is out of scope
  • Simple diary view on phone, tablet or laptop

Could have (later plans)

  • Quotes drafted from customer photos
  • Deposits and payment chasing
  • Review requests and social posts
  • A weekly summary of jobs and money owed

Won’t do

  • Book outside the owner’s rules
  • Make up prices or promise times not in the diary
  • Reply as a person; it always speaks for the business

05 / USER STORIES

Written so they can be tested

IDUser storyAcceptance criteria
JD-01As a trades owner, I want missed calls to get an instant text so the customer doesn’t ring someone else.Given a missed call in working hours, when the call ends, then the caller gets a text in the business’s name within 1 minute.
JD-02As a customer, I want to book by WhatsApp without waiting for a call back.Given a new enquiry, when the customer gives the job, postcode and preferred day, then they’re offered at least two real free slots.
JD-03As a trades owner, I want JobDesk to book only within my rules so I stay in control.Given a postcode outside the owner’s area, when the customer asks to book, then no slot is offered and the owner is notified.
JD-04As a trades owner, I want to approve bookings until I trust it.Given approval mode is on, when a customer picks a slot, then the owner gets a job card and the booking confirms only after one tap.
JD-05As a customer, I want a reminder so I don’t miss the visit.Given a confirmed job, when it is the day before, then the customer receives a reminder with the time and job summary.

06 / BUSINESS CASE

Worth it if it saves one job a month

I built an interactive calculator into the live page so each owner can test the value with their own numbers. With a modest example:

Input5 missed enquiries a week, £150 average job, 40% would have booked
Work at riskAbout £1,299 a month
Price£49 a month, so one saved job more than covers it

Modelled example, not a measured result. Real figures will come from the founding customers.

07 / TESTING AND LAUNCH

Test cases for the awkward moments, then a small launch

  • Out of hours A 10pm message gets a reply and next-day slots, not a promise for tonight
  • Out of area No booking offered; the owner decides
  • Double booking A slot taken by another customer is never offered
  • Changed mind The customer can move or cancel, and the diary updates
  • Out of scope Questions about price exceptions or emergencies are handed to the owner

Launch plan: start with a small group of founding customers in approval mode, review every conversation with them weekly, then switch on auto-confirm only when each owner is comfortable.

How success will be measured: time to first reply, share of enquiries that become bookings, owner overrides (a trust signal), and evening admin time saved.

08 / WHAT I LEARNED

Trust decides adoption

The hardest requirement wasn’t technical. It was making owners comfortable letting something reply in their name. Approval mode, clear rules and a simple handover are what make the AI usable, which is the same lesson I apply in every AI project.
