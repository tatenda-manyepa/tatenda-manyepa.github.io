Architecture Decision Records

Decisions, Trade-offs & Rationale

Architecture is about making informed trade-offs. These records explain the key choices behind the target solution, the alternatives considered, why each approach was selected and what would cause the decision to be revisited.

Decision Framework

Each decision starts with a requirement rather than a technology. Options are compared against operational resilience, maintainability, security, delivery complexity, cost and future change.

01 — NEEDWhat problem must the architecture solve?
02 — OPTIONSWhat realistic approaches were considered?
03 — DECISIONWhat was selected and why?
04 — TRADE-OFFWhat is accepted and when should it be reconsidered?

ADR-01Container Hosting — Why ECS Fargate?

Need

Reliable container hosting for application services without creating unnecessary infrastructure administration.

Options considered

Amazon ECS on Fargate, Amazon EKS/Kubernetes, or containers hosted on EC2.

Decision

Use ECS Fargate for the target application services. It provides managed container execution while reducing host and cluster-management overhead.

Trade-off accepted

Less low-level control and greater AWS dependency than a Kubernetes-based platform.

Why it fits

The requirement is resilient application hosting and operational simplicity, not Kubernetes administration. This keeps the platform proportionate to the business need.

Reconsider if

Kubernetes portability becomes a firm requirement, organisational Kubernetes capability matures, or workload economics make Fargate unsuitable.

ADR-02Operational Data — Why RDS Multi-AZ?

Need

Reduce manual database failover and improve availability for operational application data.

Options considered

Managed relational database with Multi-AZ resilience, self-managed database infrastructure, or retaining the existing database operating model.

Decision

Use Amazon RDS with Multi-AZ resilience for managed relational data and automated failover capability.

Trade-off accepted

Managed services reduce infrastructure control and can increase platform dependency.

Why it fits

The current-state problem includes operational overhead and resilience concerns. Managed database capabilities reduce routine administration while improving recovery characteristics.

Reconsider if

Application requirements demand database features, performance characteristics or deployment control that the selected managed service cannot provide.

ADR-03Integration — Why Managed Messaging?

Need

Support reliable service integration while reducing migration risk and preserving compatible messaging patterns during modernisation.

Options considered

Managed compatible messaging such as Amazon MQ, a redesigned event architecture, or continued self-management of messaging infrastructure.

Decision

Introduce managed messaging incrementally, using Amazon MQ or an appropriate event pattern where compatibility and workflow requirements justify it.

Trade-off accepted

This does not immediately redesign every integration into a fully event-native architecture.

Why it fits

Incremental integration lowers migration risk and allows legacy-compatible workflows to coexist with modernised services.

Reconsider if

Event volumes, coupling, integration requirements or future workflows justify moving individual integrations to a different event-driven pattern.

ADR-04Enterprise AI — Why Retrieval-Augmented Generation?

Need

Answer operational questions using approved enterprise knowledge while preserving source grounding and reviewability.

Options considered

Model-only generation, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), conventional search, or manually maintained knowledge lookup.

Decision

Use retrieval-augmented generation so responses are grounded in governed knowledge sources and can include source references.

Trade-off accepted

RAG adds ingestion, retrieval, evaluation and governance complexity and still requires quality controls.

Why it fits

Operational advice must be explainable and grounded. Retrieval gives the model relevant approved context rather than relying solely on model knowledge.

Reconsider if

The use case becomes deterministic enough for conventional search/workflows, or evaluation shows retrieval is not improving answer quality sufficiently.

ADR-05Foundation Models — Why Amazon Bedrock?

Need

Provide managed foundation-model access without operating dedicated model-serving infrastructure.

Options considered

Amazon Bedrock, externally hosted model APIs, or self-hosted/open model infrastructure.

Decision

Use Amazon Bedrock as the managed model-access layer for the prototype and target AI capability.

Trade-off accepted

Managed convenience introduces platform dependency and requires ongoing model, cost and service evaluation.

Why it fits

The solution needs governed model access more than it needs ownership of model-serving infrastructure.

Reconsider if

Model requirements, economics, portability, latency or governance needs favour another hosting approach.

ADR-06Observability — Why CloudWatch?

Need

Move from reactive troubleshooting toward central logs, metrics, alarms and operational visibility.

Options considered

AWS-native observability, third-party observability tooling, or continuation of fragmented/manual monitoring.

Decision

Use Amazon CloudWatch as the baseline for AWS workload logs, metrics, dashboards and alarms, with notification integration where required.

Trade-off accepted

AWS-native monitoring increases platform coupling and may not replace every specialist observability requirement.

Why it fits

Observability is required from the start so failures can be detected and investigated with searchable evidence rather than relying on manual discovery.

Reconsider if

Cross-platform observability, advanced analytics, scale or operational requirements justify a dedicated external observability platform.

ADR-07Infrastructure Delivery — Why Terraform?

Need

Make infrastructure repeatable, reviewable and less dependent on manual configuration.

Options considered

Terraform, AWS-native infrastructure-as-code tooling, or manual console configuration.

Decision

Use Terraform to define the representative AWS infrastructure as code and support repeatable deployment.

Trade-off accepted

Infrastructure as code introduces state management, versioning and delivery discipline that must itself be governed.

Why it fits

Repeatability and reviewability reduce configuration drift and make architectural intent visible in deployable infrastructure definitions.

Reconsider if

Organisational standards mandate another IaC platform or the delivery environment creates stronger benefits for an alternative.

ADR-08AI Governance — Why Human Review?

Need

Use AI to accelerate investigation without allowing uncertain model output to become operational authority.

Options considered

Fully autonomous actions, AI recommendations with human approval, or no generative-AI assistance.

Decision

Keep AI advisory. It retrieves evidence, assists analysis and produces recommendations, while people remain responsible for validation and operational action.

Trade-off accepted

Human review limits the maximum automation achievable and may add time compared with autonomous execution.

Why it fits

Accuracy, explainability and safe adoption matter more than full automation in high-impact operational workflows.

Reconsider if

A narrowly defined action becomes sufficiently deterministic, low-risk and well-controlled to justify increased automation with appropriate safeguards.
